Open questions raised by the build

These are points where the prototype had to choose because the specification does not. Each one is a candidate for a clarification request before implementation.

  1. Four requirement IDs, one sentence. BASE-R02, R04, R05 and R07 quote the same overview sentence verbatim. As distinct requirements they are not separable and cannot be independently tested — a workflow that passes one passes all four for the same reason.
  2. Duplicate numbering in Iteration 10. I10-R01 through I10-R04 each appear twice with different text. The second set reads as a restatement, but nothing declares which is normative, so an implementer choosing one is guessing.
  3. Iteration 2 is amended twice and never restated. I07 makes late cancellations billable, reversing "cancellations are not billable"; I08 makes terminal states reversible, reversing "no further transition is permitted". Anyone reading I02 in isolation implements the wrong system. The prototype implements the amended rules and marks I02 accordingly.
  4. Iteration 6 migration fabricates rates it calls historical. Dividing an existing invoice total by its lesson count preserves the total but invents a per-line rate that was never in force — most visibly for an invoice mixing a full-rate lesson with a 50% late-cancel charge. The prototype flags every migrated line not historically verified; the specification does not say whether that is acceptable.
  5. Late-cancel boundary. "At or after the window boundary is a late cancellation" reads as inclusive, so the prototype treats exactly 24 hours before start as late. If the intent is exclusive, one test case flips.
  6. Reversion of a co-billed lesson. I08 says the covering unpaid invoice is voided in full and co-billed lessons return to Unbilled. It does not say whether the student is notified that an invoice they were sent no longer stands. The prototype does not email; that is a decision, not a reading.
  7. Iteration 5 has no readable prompt. Its narrative is struck through in the reviewed document; only I05-R01 through R03 survive. The delivery event vocabulary used here (queued → sent → delivered → opened / failed) is inferred from the requirement text and from downstream "preserved behaviour" clauses, not stated anywhere.
  8. Monthly billing and lesson state. I06 gives monthly invoices a single period line with no lesson identifiers. Nothing says what happens to a completed lesson belonging to a monthly student — whether it stays in Unbilled Lessons forever or is consumed by the period. The prototype keeps monthly students' lessons out of per-lesson invoicing but does not clear them.

What is real and what is not

  • Real: identifier assignment and backfill, lifecycle transitions and their guards, late-cancel arithmetic, billing eligibility, invoice line composition and totals, voiding, reversion, snapshot bounding, note visibility, portal scoping and invitation single-use. All of it runs in js/engine.js and can be exercised through the interface.
  • Not real: there is no server, no database and no Stripe. "Pay with Stripe" settles the invoice immediately. Email is recorded as a notification record rather than sent — the Email activity screen is the outbox. State lives in memory: reloading the page reseeds the studio.
